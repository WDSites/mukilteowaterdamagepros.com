Standing water is an emergency, so start here

Water on the floor is moving into your subfloor, baseboards and walls every minute it stays. Emergency water removal is the pump-and-extract step that gets the bulk of it out. The dispatch line is open 24/7, so night and weekend calls are answered like any other.

Before help arrives, find the source and stop it. A burst supply line has a shutoff under the fixture, and the main valve is the backup. Turn off power to the wet area only if you can do it from dry ground, and lift furniture, rugs and electronics out of the water.

If the water is near electricity or sewage

Stay out, and do not wade in. Call 911 or your electric utility for any electrical or gas risk before you call us. If the water came from a toilet, drain or sewer line, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Burst pipe water damage: the first hour

A burst pipe under a sink or in a wall can release a lot of water fast. The first job is the shutoff, and the second is getting a crew on the water before it spreads under cabinets and into the subfloor.

Common sources include supply lines that fail at fittings, frozen pipes that split, water heater tanks, and washing machine hoses. Photograph the area before moving anything, since your insurer will want to see how it looked.

How emergency water extraction works

Crews use submersible pumps for deep water and truck-mounted or portable extraction units for carpet and hard floors. After the bulk of the water is gone, moisture meters check what remains in the floor, walls and cabinets.

  • Pumps and extraction units remove standing water and soaked carpet water.
  • Wet carpet pad is usually removed, because it holds water that carpet extraction cannot reach.
  • Furniture is lifted or blocked so legs do not stain wet floors.
  • Moisture checks show whether drying is needed next.

Extraction is the start, not the finish. Wet structure still needs water mitigation with air movers and dehumidifiers, and that is where most of the drying happens.

Clean water, gray water and sewage change the plan

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water into three categories. The category decides what can be dried and what must be removed.

Water categories and what they mean
CategoryTypical sourceWhat usually happens
1, cleanBroken supply line, sink overflow with clean waterExtract and dry, most materials can stay
2, grayWashing machine or dishwasher dischargeExtract, remove porous materials, sanitize
3, blackSewage backup, rising floodwaterRemove affected materials, sanitize, see flood damage restoration

Clean water can become gray if it sits. That is another reason to call early rather than wait to see if it dries on its own.

What the cost depends on

Dispatch explains scope and price before work starts. Extraction cost follows these factors.

  • How much water there is and how deep it is.
  • Water category, since gray and black water add disposal and sanitizing.
  • Floor type, because carpet, hardwood and tile need different handling.
  • Access, such as stairs, crawl spaces or a finished basement.
  • Whether drying and repair follow, which are separate steps.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners and renters policies, while gradual leaks and flood damage often are not. Check your policy, and keep photos and receipts. Ask for your real number at (425) 592-3399.

After the water is out

Once the standing water is gone, the structure is still wet. The next move is usually water damage cleanup to clear debris and sanitize, then mitigation to dry the frame and subfloor. Skipping those steps is the most common reason mold shows up weeks later.
