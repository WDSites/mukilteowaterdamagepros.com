Carpet Water Damage in Mukilteo, WA

Wet carpet can often be saved if the water was clean and drying starts fast, but the pad underneath usually tells the real story.

☎ Call (425) 592-3399

Why carpet holds water longer than it looks

Carpet is a three-part system: the fibers on top, a backing under them and a foam or fiber pad beneath. Water passes through the surface in seconds and settles in the pad, which acts like a sponge. A rug that feels only damp on top may have a soaked pad and a wet subfloor below.

In Mukilteo, where winters are mild and rainy, carpet sits in bedrooms, stairs and finished lower levels that rarely get airflow. A slow leak or a tracked-in puddle can sit under the pad for days before anyone notices. By then the question has shifted from drying to deciding what is worth keeping.

Safety steps for a flooded carpeted room

  1. Turn off the water source at the fixture or the main valve if you can reach it safely.
  2. If water reaches outlets, power strips or a panel, stay out of the room and cut power only from a dry spot.
  3. Smell gas or see sparks? Leave and call 911 and your gas utility before you call anyone else.
  4. Move electronics, books and furniture legs off the wet carpet and set aluminum foil or blocks under heavy pieces.
  5. Do not run a household vacuum on wet carpet, and do not use a space heater near it.
  6. Call your water district or Snohomish County PUD if the leak involves a main line or live electrical equipment.

When carpet can dry in place and when it goes

The water category drives the decision. Clean supply water, called Category 1 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, often leaves carpet that can be cleaned and dried. Category 2 and Category 3 water are different, because they carry contamination that does not rinse out of fibers.

EPA guidance tells people to throw away anything that cannot be cleaned and dried, and says porous items like carpet may have to go if they turn moldy. Age matters too. Carpet that has been wet for more than a day or two with a saturated pad is often a removal job even when the first water was clean.

Dry in place

Clean water, fast call, pad still intact. The crew lifts and floats the carpet to dry the pad and subfloor.

Save carpet, replace pad

Pad is soaked but the carpet fibers are clean. The pad is removed and discarded, and the carpet is cleaned.

Remove all

Contaminated water or mold growth. Carpet, pad and tack strip come out and the subfloor is cleaned.

The drying setup crews use on carpeted floors

A technician starts with a moisture meter and a thermal camera to map the wet area, since carpet can hide moisture well beyond the visible stain. Extraction units then pull the standing and surface water out, which removes far more water than fans alone. Air movers push dry air across the surface while LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the room.

On a floated carpet job, the crew detaches one edge from the tack strip, props the carpet on a blower and pushes warm air under it. That dries the pad or the subfloor without tearing everything out. Afterward the carpet is re-stretched and reattached, and a local carpet installer can finish that step if needed.

Stairs, basements and tight rooms

Stairs are tricky because water runs down the treads and soaks each step in turn. Crews often drill small ports or lift the nosing to reach the cavity underneath. Carpet over a concrete slab, common in lower levels, wicks moisture upward and can hold it against the slab for weeks.

When water has climbed the wall, the baseboard and the lower drywall may need to come off as well. Our water damage repair crew rebuilds those pieces after drying is confirmed.

What changes the price of a carpet job

Scope and price are explained before work starts. The main factors that move the total are listed below.

  • The square footage of carpet and pad that got wet.
  • The water category and how long it sat.
  • Whether the carpet is glued over a slab, stretched over a pad or wall-to-wall with stairs.
  • Whether the subfloor, tack strip or baseboard also need drying or replacement.
  • How many days of equipment the room needs, which depends on materials and conditions.

Who owns the carpet and who pays

According to Census Bureau QuickFacts for Mukilteo, about 70.6 percent of homes are owner-occupied, which leaves roughly 3 in 10 occupied homes as rentals. If you rent, the carpet may belong to your landlord. Tell the landlord in writing before anything is thrown out, and check your lease and your renters policy.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, and gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer about carpet, pad and flooring. The Washington Insurance Commissioner advises taking many photos and keeping small samples of the materials you discard for the adjuster.

Common mistakes with wet carpet

  • Waiting to see if it dries on its own while the pad stays wet.
  • Steam cleaning over a soaked pad, which pushes more water down.
  • Throwing out the carpet before photographing it.
  • Using bleach on stains, which does not remove contamination.
  • Pulling carpet up in a hurry and leaving the pad behind, which can leave moisture trapped against the subfloor.

After the carpet is dry

A dry carpet still needs a moisture check at the subfloor and along the baseboard, since surface dry and structure dry are different things. EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold, and the clock starts when the water arrives. If mold does show up, our mold remediation work follows ANSI/IICRC S520.
