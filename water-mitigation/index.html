When water is an emergency in your Mukilteo home

If water is actively moving through a floor, wall or ceiling, treat it as an emergency. Water mitigation is the work that stops the damage from growing, and it is time sensitive. Dispatch is open 24/7, so a call at 2 a.m. gets answered the same as a call at noon.

While you wait for a crew, stop the source if you can. Shut the main water valve, or the shutoff under the fixture. Turn off power to the wet area only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground. Lift valuables off wet floors and keep people and pets out of any water that may have touched outlets.

Electrical and gas come first

If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, stay out and call 911 or your electric utility before you call us. Snohomish County PUD serves electric in Mukilteo and Puget Sound Energy serves gas, according to the City of Mukilteo's utilities page.

Carpet is squishy, a wall feels damp, and you do not know what to call this

Most people do not search for water mitigation. They search for a soaked carpet, a damp wall, or a ceiling stain that keeps growing. The trade name for the fix is water mitigation: finding where the water went and removing it before it does more harm.

Mitigation is different from rebuilding. It comes first, and it decides how much of your home has to be replaced later. A wall that is dried in time can stay. A wall that sits wet for a week usually cannot.

What water mitigation includes, step by step

Crews that follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration work in a set order. You can ask any company whether its crews work to S500. A typical job follows these steps.

  1. Stop or isolate the water source, and identify the water category (clean, gray or black).
  2. Extract standing water with pumps and extraction units.
  3. Map the moisture with meters and a thermal camera, so wet areas behind finishes are found, not guessed.
  4. Remove materials that cannot be dried safely, such as saturated carpet pad or soft drywall.
  5. Set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, then monitor readings until the structure is dry.
  6. Document the loss with photos and moisture logs for your insurance claim.

Structural drying: what each machine is doing

Structural drying is the longest part of mitigation. It uses equipment that you may hear running for several days. Drying time depends on the materials and conditions, and typically runs a few days.

Common drying equipment
EquipmentWhat it doesWhy it is on your job
Moisture meterReads moisture inside wood, drywall and concreteShows what is wet, and later proves what is dry
Thermal cameraFinds cool, wet areas behind surfacesLocates hidden water without opening every wall
Air moverPushes air across wet surfacesSpeeds evaporation from floors and wall bases
LGR dehumidifierPulls moisture out of the airKeeps evaporated water from settling elsewhere
Extraction unitPulls standing water from floors and carpetRemoves the bulk of the water before drying starts

Drying equipment is set up and monitored by the crew, because placement and daily readings decide whether the structure actually dries. Ask dispatch what is included in your scope.

Why the first 24 to 72 hours matter in a wet climate

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Mukilteo has mild, rainy winters, so outdoor air rarely helps. Opening windows in January usually adds humidity instead of removing it, which is why dehumidifiers do the work.

Waiting also changes the water category. Clean supply-line water can become contaminated as it sits, and contaminated materials are removed rather than dried. A quick call keeps more of your home in place.

Mitigation, cleanup or repair: which do you need?

Many jobs need all three, in order. Mitigation comes first and stops the damage. Water damage cleanup removes debris and sanitizes after the water is controlled, and water damage repair rebuilds what could not be saved.

What affects the cost of water mitigation

Dispatch explains scope and price before work starts, and the final number depends on your loss. These factors move it most.

  • Water category: clean water is the simplest, and gray or black water adds removal and sanitizing.
  • Affected area: more rooms and more wall height mean more equipment and more days.
  • Materials: carpet, hardwood, drywall and insulation dry very differently.
  • Drying days and equipment count, set by daily moisture readings.
  • Rebuild scope afterward, which is a separate step.

Insurance is a separate question. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage.
