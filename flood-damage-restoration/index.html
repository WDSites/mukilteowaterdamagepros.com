When flood water or sewage is an emergency

Flood damage is different from a broken pipe. Water that enters from outside or backs up from a drain often carries soil, bacteria and debris, so it is handled as contaminated. Dispatch is open 24/7 for flood calls, including storms that hit at night.

While you wait, keep everyone out of the water. Do not use household vacuums on it, and do not walk through it near outlets. Shut off power to the affected area only if you can reach the panel while dry, and call 911 or your utility for any electrical or gas risk first.

Sewage and floodwater are not a do-it-yourself job

The EPA advises calling a professional with experience in contaminated water when the water came from sewage or other contaminated sources. Gloves alone are not enough, and porous materials that touched this water are usually removed.

How flood restoration works

Flood work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. You can ask any company whether its crews work to S500. The steps are close to other water jobs, with more removal and sanitizing.

  1. Assess the water category and the extent of the flood, including crawl spaces and wall cavities.
  2. Extract water and remove contaminated carpet, pad, insulation and wet drywall where needed.
  3. Clean and sanitize hard surfaces and framing that stay.
  4. Dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers, with daily moisture readings.
  5. Document the loss, then move on to repair.

If the water is in a basement

Flood water settles in a basement first, and finished basements hold it in carpet, drywall and stored belongings. Crews extract the water, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the slab and walls to normal readings. Restoration is not waterproofing, so ask a drainage contractor to fix the cause. Details are on the flooded basement cleanup page.

If the water is sewage

A sewage backup is Category 3 water. Stay out of the area, do not run water, and check whether the backup comes from your own line or the public sewer, since that changes who is responsible. Check your policy, because sewer backup coverage often depends on an endorsement. The full process is on the sewage backup cleanup page.

What affects the cost of flood restoration

Dispatch explains scope and price before work starts. These factors drive it.

  • Water category, since contaminated water adds removal and sanitizing.
  • Affected square footage and how high the water rose on walls.
  • Materials that must be removed and disposed of.
  • Drying days and equipment count.
  • Rebuild scope once the structure is dry.

Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowners and renters policies and needs separate flood coverage. The Washington Insurance Commissioner's flood guidance advises photographing the damage when it is safe, keeping small samples of discarded materials for the adjuster, and asking your insurer when it can inspect. Ask your insurer what applies to you.
