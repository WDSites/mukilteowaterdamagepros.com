How the Bowl, the waterfront and the outer neighborhoods meet water

Downtown Edmonds sits in a valley that locals call the Bowl. Water runs downhill, so rain and runoff from the slopes around it tend to end up on the lower streets, and basements and ground-floor shops on those blocks are the first places to collect it. If your building sits low in the Bowl, check the FEMA flood map for your address and keep a wet vacuum and a few sandbags where you can reach them.

The waterfront is a different problem. The ferry terminal at the west end of Main Street and the Brackett's Landing shoreline mean salt air, tidal moisture and a lot of commercial space close to the water. If a waterfront unit or shop floods, give dispatch the nearest cross street or the ferry terminal as a reference point, because that is the quickest way to place you.

Edmonds Marsh Park is a 28-acre saltwater marsh south of downtown, one of the few left in the state. Low ground near a marsh holds water longer after heavy rain than ground on a ridge. If you live in that area, watch for damp crawl spaces and soft floors, and call early when you see standing water rather than waiting for it to drain.

Five Corners, Westgate and Perrinville each have their own shopping centers and a mix of older and newer homes. Slopes and driveways pitch water toward foundations, garages and lower levels. Walk the downhill side of your house after a storm and look for pooling near the foundation.

The wet-season pattern of Puget Sound winters

Puget Sound winters are mild and rainy, and a long run of wet days can load gutters, drains and yards. The common winter calls are roof and gutter overflow, backed-up drains, sump pump failure and water seeping into lower levels. Clear gutters and downspouts before October, and test your sump pump with a bucket of water.

Summer is drier, but pipes, water heaters and washing machine hoses fail in any month. A slow leak behind a wall can run for weeks before a stain shows. If you smell must or see a ceiling stain, call before the damage spreads. If mold has already started, see our mold remediation page.

What happens after you call

  1. Dispatch takes your address, the water source and any safety concerns.
  2. A crew checks moisture levels with meters and a thermal camera.
  3. We extract standing water and set up air movers and dehumidifiers.
  4. We remove wet materials that cannot be saved, such as soaked insulation and drywall.
  5. We monitor drying, which typically takes a few days and depends on materials and conditions.

Ask any company whether its crews follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage. When the building is dry, we can handle repair work such as drywall, flooring and paint.

What affects the cost of a water loss

  • The water category, since sewage and floodwater need more protection and disposal.
  • How much area is wet and how many materials are involved.
  • How long the water sat before extraction began.
  • Whether wet drywall, insulation or flooring has to come out.
  • How many days of drying equipment the building needs.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowner policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph the damage when it is safe.

City utilities, permits and who to call

Edmonds runs its own water, sewer and stormwater utilities through Public Works and Utilities, and wastewater goes to the regional Edmonds Wastewater Treatment Plant. If the problem is in the street or the city main, call the city. If it is a private line on your property, that part is yours, and we can help once you have shut off your side.

Your stormwater utility bill is a quick way to confirm you are in the city system. Permit questions go to the Planning and Development counter at City Hall, which is closed on Wednesdays, so plan rebuild paperwork around that. Edmonds was incorporated in 1890, and older buildings can hide older plumbing, so ask the city what applies to your address.
