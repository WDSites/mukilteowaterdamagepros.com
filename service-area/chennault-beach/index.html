Water damage in Chennault Beach

Chennault Beach is a residential area in southwest Mukilteo, named for Chennault Beach Road and Chennault Beach Drive. Listings show mostly detached single-family houses on lots that range from small to over an acre, with some condos near Harbour Pointe Boulevard. Local guides describe west-facing, wooded streets.

The boundary between Chennault Beach, Harbour Pointe and the unincorporated Picnic Point area to the south is not marked on any official map we found. If you are unsure whether your address is in the City of Mukilteo, call and we will help sort it out.

Slopes, trees and storm water

Wooded west slopes shed rain downhill. The City of Mukilteo's surface water plan lists Chennault Beach as one of its drainage basins and notes many areas of localized flooding citywide. A drainage project on Chennault Beach Drive has been listed for bid, so check with the city for current status.

Heavy rain can push water into garages, crawl spaces and low rooms through foundation edges and window wells. Roof gutters that overflow can send water against siding. After a storm, look for wet walls, damp carpet edges and musty smells.

  • Water entering a garage or crawl space after heavy rain.
  • Clogged gutters sending water down walls.
  • Roof leaks under wind-driven rain.
  • Supply line, water heater and appliance leaks inside the house.

For storm-driven water, see storm water damage. If the lowest level is wet, read about basement water damage. Cleanup after the water is out is covered on water damage cleanup.

Flood zones

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified for Chennault Beach. The City's plan says mapped floodplain is limited to a small section of the shoreline. Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for an address. Zone X is not a guarantee against water.

If water is spreading in Chennault Beach, do this first

Stop the source

Shut off the fixture valve or the main water valve. If you cannot find it, leave the area and call your water utility.

Power only if it is safe

Never touch a breaker or switch while standing in water or on a wet floor. If water is near the panel or outlets, stay out and call the utility or 911.

Protect people and pets

Keep everyone out of rooms with sagging ceilings or standing water. Sewage or flood water is not safe to touch.

Document, then call

Take photos of the damage and the source before you move anything. Then call (425) 592-3399 and describe what you see.

What restoration involves

A crew finds the source, removes standing water and maps moisture in walls and floors. Wet drywall and insulation may be removed, and structural materials are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard, and you can ask any provider how its work follows it.

Read water mitigation for the drying steps and emergency water removal for active water. Our flood damage restoration page covers larger events.

Mold and contaminated water

Damp, shaded spaces can grow mold if wet materials are not dried. No company can guarantee mold will not return. Sewage or floodwater should not be dried with household fans or a shop vacuum. Call for professional handling.

Insurance questions

Take dated photos, keep receipts and ask your insurer what your policy covers. The insurer decides coverage. Ask about deductibles and required steps before work starts.
