Water damage in Harbour Pointe

Harbour Pointe was annexed into the City of Mukilteo in 1991 and roughly doubled its size. It was built as a planned community with homes, schools, a golf course, parks and shopping. Local housing guides list a median build year near 1992, mostly detached houses with some townhomes and condos.

Our own office is on Harbour Heights Pkwy in Mukilteo, so this is not a far-off service call. Homes here include subdivisions such as Discovery Crest, with detached houses built around 1990. These pages avoid guessing about any single street.

Water problems in homes of this era

Houses built in the early 1990s now have decades on roofs, water heaters, supply lines and appliances. That does not mean they fail, but wear is a common source of slow leaks. A small stain on a ceiling or a damp baseboard is worth checking early.

  • Roof or flashing leaks that show up as ceiling stains after steady rain.
  • Water heater leaks in a garage, closet or utility room.
  • Washing machine, dishwasher and refrigerator line leaks.
  • Toilet, sink and shower supply failures on upper floors that soak the ceiling below.

Roof water is covered on our roof leak water damage page. For a tank that failed, see water heater leak cleanup. A wet kitchen is covered under kitchen water damage.

Drainage and wet ground

The City of Mukilteo's surface water plan names Big Gulch, Chennault Beach and other drainage basins, and it describes the Harbour Pointe wetlands as regional detention. Planned drainage does not mean every lot stays dry. The same plan notes many areas of localized flooding across the city.

We do not claim any Harbour Pointe street or lot is or is not in a flood zone. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for an address, and remember that Zone X is not a guarantee.

If water is spreading in Harbour Pointe, do this first

Stop the source

Shut off the fixture valve or the main water valve. If you cannot find it, leave the area and call your water utility.

Power only if it is safe

Never touch a breaker or switch while standing in water or on a wet floor. If water is near the panel or outlets, stay out and call the utility or 911.

Protect people and pets

Keep everyone out of rooms with sagging ceilings or standing water. Sewage or flood water is not safe to touch.

Document, then call

Take photos of the damage and the source before you move anything. Then call (425) 592-3399 and describe what you see.

How the work goes

A crew finds the source, extracts standing water and uses moisture meters to map wet drywall, insulation and flooring. Materials that can be dried are dried and materials that cannot are removed. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any provider how it applies.

See water mitigation for the drying steps and water damage cleanup for removal and sanitizing. Rebuilding is covered on the water damage repair page.

Mold and contaminated water

If wet materials stay damp for days, mold can start. Our mold remediation page explains what is involved. No restoration company can guarantee mold will not return. Sewage or floodwater should never be handled with household fans or a shop vacuum.

Insurance questions

Document the damage with dated photos and keep receipts for emergency steps. Your insurer decides what is covered under your policy. Ask about deductibles, required steps and the claim process before work starts.
