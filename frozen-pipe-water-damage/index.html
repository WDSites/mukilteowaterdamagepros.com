Ice rarely does its damage while it is solid. The trouble starts at the thaw, when a cracked pipe begins to run again.

Frozen pipes in a mild climate

Freezing is not a daily worry here the way it is in Minnesota or Montana. Most winters on the Puget Sound shoreline bring only a handful of hard-freeze nights, and many homes were never built with deep cold in mind. That is exactly why a freeze catches people off guard, since the pipes are often routed through spaces nobody insulated.

The weak spots are predictable: crawl spaces, detached garages, exterior walls on the north side, outdoor hose bibs and unheated additions or bonus rooms. Ice expands inside the pipe and can split it along a seam or push a fitting apart. You usually do not see anything until the ice melts and water starts moving through the crack.

How often it actually gets cold enough

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 climate normals for the Everett Snohomish County airport station at Paine Field show an average of 28.8 days a year with a low at or below 32 degrees. Those days cluster in December at 8.3, January at 7.3 and February at 6.6. So if you live in Mukilteo, the practical step is to walk your crawl space and garage in November, before the December nights arrive.

Cold weather is not always a long, steady freeze, either. The Office of the Washington State Climatologist reported freezing rain across the Puget Sound region on December 23, 2022, with western Washington running 1 to 6 degrees below normal that month. A few unusual nights are enough to catch an unprepared pipe, which is why a plan for the next one is worth having.

Safety steps when the thaw begins

  1. Close the main water valve before you do anything else. Take the handle all the way to the stop.
  2. Keep the heat on and open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so room air can reach the pipes.
  3. If water is near outlets, a breaker panel or a ceiling fixture, keep out of it. Cut power only if you can reach the panel while dry.
  4. If you smell gas or something sparks, leave, then call 911 and Puget Sound Energy or Snohomish County PUD from outside.
  5. Never use a torch, a propane heater or an open flame on a frozen pipe. A hair dryer on low is the most you should try, and only on a pipe that is not yet leaking.
  6. Call a plumber for the repair. We do not fix pipes, but we coordinate with the plumber.

What our crew does once the water is running

A frozen-pipe leak is often in a place nobody was looking, so the first job is finding where the water went. A thermal camera highlights cooler, wet surfaces behind drywall and in ceilings, and a moisture meter confirms how deep the wetness runs in studs, plywood and subfloor. Standing water goes out through extraction units.

After that, air movers drive dry air across wet surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air. Insulation soaked in a wall or crawl space is often removed, because it holds water and dries slowly. If a ceiling is already sagging, that section usually comes down so the cavity can dry properly.

Readings guide when the equipment comes out. Typically a few days, depending on materials and conditions. You can ask any company whether it works to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. Our water damage cleanup page covers the removal and sanitizing work that follows.

What drives the cost after a freeze

  • How long the pipe ran after the thaw before someone noticed
  • Whether the break was in an open crawl space or inside a finished wall
  • How many rooms and levels the water reached
  • How much insulation, drywall or flooring has to come out
  • Whether the vacant or seasonal home sat unattended for days
  • How many days the equipment needs to run

A break caught quickly usually costs far less than one found after a weekend away. We explain the scope and price before work starts, so you see the numbers first.

Frozen pipes and your policy

The Washington Insurance Commissioner says homeowners coverage usually includes burst pipes, frozen pipes among them, as long as you heat your home properly. That condition matters, because a home left unheated for a long stretch may be treated differently. Check your policy and ask your insurer what it expects, then photograph the damage before anything is cleared out.

Prevention before the next cold snap

Alderwood Water and Wastewater District's published frost guidance says to take hoses off outdoor spigots, because water trapped in the faucet can freeze and burst the pipe. It also points to draining or covering spigots, blowing out irrigation lines and sealing cold-air gaps. Customers of another district should ask their own water utility for its advice.

  • Disconnect and store garden hoses before the first cold forecast
  • Insulate pipes in the crawl space, garage and exterior walls
  • Seal gaps where cold air reaches pipes, such as vents and cable holes
  • Let a faucet drip on the coldest nights if the pipe runs along an outside wall
  • Leave heat on at 55 degrees or warmer when you travel
  • Learn where your main shutoff is, so you can reach it in the dark
