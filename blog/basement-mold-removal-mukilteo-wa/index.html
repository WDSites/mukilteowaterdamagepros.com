Basement Mold Removal in Mukilteo, WA: DIY or Call a Pro?

A clear line between a weekend cleanup and a job that needs containment, plus what each path involves.

By the Mukilteo Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026

☎ Call (425) 592-3399

The 10 square foot line

Mold under about 10 square feet, roughly a 3 foot by 3 foot patch, is usually a do-it-yourself job, according to the EPA. Call a professional when it covers more than that, when a lot of water got in, or when the water was sewage or otherwise contaminated. Either way, fix the moisture source first, because mold needs moisture to grow.

This post covers the gear, the steps for a small patch, what a professional crew does, and how clearance testing fits in. Health questions about mold exposure belong to a physician.

If you go the DIY route

The EPA recommends gloves, goggles and an N-95 respirator for a small cleanup. Stop the water first, then dry the area. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours.

  1. Close the basement door and keep air from blowing across the moldy surface toward other rooms.
  2. Bag porous items such as carpet, ceiling tiles or soaked cardboard, since they may have to be thrown away.
  3. Scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water, then wipe them dry.
  4. Dry the whole area completely and keep watching it for new dampness.

The EPA does not recommend bleach as a routine cleanup method. Never mix bleach with ammonia, since the mix can release dangerous gas. Do not paint over the spot, because paint hides mold and does nothing about the moisture.

Stop and call a pro if the patch turns out bigger once you start scrubbing, or if the wall behind it is soft or wet. Surprises like that are common, since moisture hides behind paneling and under flooring. It is better to stop early than to spread material around a room you are trying to clean.

What a professional job looks like

A crew working to the published ANSI/IICRC S520 standard follows a set order. If the basement is still wet, water mitigation comes first, so the moisture is under control before mold work starts. The steps below are how a typical mold remediation job is laid out.

  1. Moisture mapping with meters and a thermal camera to find the source and how far it spread.
  2. Containment with plastic barriers around the work zone.
  3. Negative air, so air moves into the work zone, with HEPA air scrubbers running.
  4. Removal of porous materials, bagged before they leave the containment.
  5. HEPA vacuuming and cleaning of the remaining surfaces.
  6. Drying with air movers and dehumidifiers until readings come down.
  7. Verification, which can be a visual check, moisture readings and sometimes testing.

A pro job also leaves a paper trail. Ask for the moisture readings taken at the start and at the end, along with a list of what was removed. Those notes help if an insurer, lender or buyer asks questions later.

Clearance testing for a lender or buyer

If a lender or buyer wants proof the work is done, the testing is usually done by an independent party, not the remediation company. That separation matters, because the company that did the work should not be the only one grading it. Ask your lender or the buyer's agent what they require before you schedule anything.

The CDC does not recommend routine mold testing, and the EPA says sampling is usually unnecessary when mold is visible. A sale can still call for it, so treat it as a paperwork question rather than a cleanup question. If you already have a deadline from a lender or a closing date, mention it on your first call so the crew can plan the work around it.

Mistakes that make it worse

Four things not to do

Do not paint over mold. Paint hides the spot and leaves the moisture in place.

Do not run a fan across moldy surfaces or open the area to other rooms. Moving air carries material into places that were clean.

Do not mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaning products. The fumes can be dangerous.

Do not wade into water that touches outlets, a panel or sewage. Call the utility or 911 about electrical or gas risk before calling the restoration line.

According to City of Mukilteo community information, the climate is mild with rainy winters and dry summers. If your home has a basement or crawl space, a below-grade room may see groundwater seepage during long wet spells. Check the walls and floor edges for dampness during the rainy season, and dry anything damp within 24 to 48 hours.
