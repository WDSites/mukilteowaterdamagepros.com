How to Remove Mold From Wood in Mukilteo, WA Homes

Surface mold on sealed wood can often be scrubbed off, but soft or deeply stained wood and structural framing call for a different plan.

By the Mukilteo Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026

☎ Call (425) 592-3399

Mold on finished wood, like trim, a table or a painted deck rail, is often a surface problem you can scrub with detergent and water. Wear gloves, goggles and an N-95 respirator, then dry the wood completely. Framing, subfloor and crawl space joists are different, and so is any patch over about 10 square feet. First find out why the wood got wet and fix that.

Finished wood versus framing and subfloor

Sealed or painted wood has a barrier on top. Mold sits on that coating and can usually be wiped or scrubbed off. Raw, unsealed wood is porous, so growth can reach below the surface, and cleaning gets harder.

Framing and subfloor add another problem: you often cannot see all of the affected surface. Joists, sill plates and plywood under flooring may be wet on the back side or hidden by insulation. If you find mold on structural wood, treat it as a moisture investigation first and a cleaning job second.

A screwdriver tells you a lot. Press the tip into the wood. If it sinks in easily or the wood crumbles, the fibers have broken down, and cleaning will not restore them.

Small-area cleanup steps

These steps fit a patch under 10 square feet on solid, sound wood, and the EPA lists this size as a typical do-it-yourself job. Larger areas, sewage-contaminated water or a lot of water damage call for a professional.

  1. Fix or stop the water source, then run fans or a dehumidifier to start drying.
  2. Put on gloves, goggles and an N-95 respirator. Keep kids and pets out of the room.
  3. Open a window if you can, and keep the cleanup area away from vents that spread dust.
  4. Mix a mild detergent with water and scrub the wood with a stiff brush or rag.
  5. Wipe the residue off with a clean damp cloth. Do not use a dry brush, which throws spores into the air.
  6. Dry the wood completely, ideally within a day or two. The EPA advises drying water-damaged items within 24 to 48 hours.
  7. Bag the rags and brushes, and wash your clothes and hands afterward.
  8. Check the wood again after a week. If it is still damp, soft or discolored, the job is not finished.

Bleach and ammonia

The EPA does not recommend bleach as a routine mold cleanup method. Never mix bleach with ammonia, since the combination makes dangerous fumes. Do not combine any cleaning products.

Sanding and HEPA vacuuming: why pros contain the area

Sanding can remove a thin layer of stained wood, but it also puts fine dust into the air. That dust can carry mold fragments through the house. Professionals handle this with containment, which means plastic barriers, negative air and air scrubbers fitted with HEPA filtration, plus protective gear for the crew.

After sanding or wire brushing, a HEPA vacuum picks up the fine particles that a regular vacuum would blow back out. A shop vacuum without a HEPA filter is not a substitute. For anything beyond a small, sealed piece of furniture, this is a reason to call for mold remediation rather than improvise in the living space.

Industry practice for mold work follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, which covers containment, removal and verification. You can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

If the wood is sound and cleaned, sealing or priming helps it resist new moisture. Sealer is a coating, not a cure, and it does nothing if water keeps reaching the board.

Decks outdoors, joists in the crawl space

Outdoor decks get wet and dry in cycles, and algae, mildew and mold often grow on shaded boards. Sweep debris from the gaps, scrub with detergent and water, rinse, and let the boards dry in the sun. Check that gutters and downspouts are not dumping water onto the deck or the footing.

Crawl spaces are harder. Joists sit in a space with little air movement, and a vapor barrier that has torn or shifted lets ground moisture rise. Look for standing water, wet insulation, soft sill plates and musty air. If you find damp insulation or water on the ground, the issue is a water damage cleanup job as much as a mold one, since wet materials may have to be removed before the framing can dry.

Stop the moisture first, then decide

Mold needs moisture, so the plan always starts with the water. Common sources on wood include roof or plumbing leaks, poor drainage near the foundation, a missing vapor barrier, condensation on cold framing and high indoor humidity. Fix the source, then dry the area fully.

Call a professional if the growth is over 10 square feet, if the wood is soft, if the water came from sewage or another contaminated source, or if a lot of water got into the structure. Mukilteo local note: rainy winters, decks and crawl spaces

Mukilteo has mild, rainy winters and dry summers, so wood outdoors and under the house spends months damp before it can dry. Use the dry months to inspect deck boards, rails and crawl space joists, and clean off surface growth early.

According to the City of Mukilteo's Permit Center page, the city lists stormwater rules in Mukilteo Municipal Code Chapter 13.12, and the Engineering group covers clearing, grading and stormwater. If a drainage fix near a deck or foundation means moving soil, call the Permit Center at 425-263-8000 before you dig. Electrical work for a crawl space light or sump pump goes through Washington L&I, not the city, so ask L&I about permits.
