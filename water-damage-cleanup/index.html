The first 48 hours after water damage

Cleanup starts when the water source is stopped and standing water is out. The first 48 hours matter because wet materials start to break down and can grow mold. Dispatch is open 24/7, so cleanup calls are answered at any hour, and same-day response is available when crews are free.

Meanwhile, take photos, lift belongings off wet floors, and keep the area ventilated if the weather allows. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water, and stay out of any room where water reached outlets until power is checked.

What water damage cleanup includes

Cleanup is the removal and cleaning stage between emergency water removal and repair. It deals with what the water left behind: wet debris, soaked soft goods, and surfaces that need cleaning. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard guides which materials can be cleaned and which should go.

  • Removal of soaked carpet pad, insulation, ceiling tile and damaged drywall.
  • Bagging and hauling away debris that cannot be saved.
  • Cleaning and sanitizing hard surfaces, framing and subfloor that stay.
  • Deodorizing, using cleaning and air filtration rather than masking smells.
  • Sorting contents into items to clean, dry or discard.

Clean, gray or black water: what gets thrown away

The water category decides most of the cleanup plan. Category 1 water from a clean supply line leaves more materials worth saving. Category 2 and 3 water usually means porous items like carpet pad, upholstery and insulation are removed.

Odors and what a smell is telling you

A musty smell after water damage means moisture is still present somewhere. Cleanup crews find the source with moisture meters and a thermal camera, then remove it. Spray fresheners cover the smell for a day and leave the damp material in place.

If the smell persists after cleanup, ask the crew to recheck readings behind baseboards and under flooring. Persistent odors often point to wet insulation or a hidden pocket of water. See mold remediation if growth is visible.

What affects the cost of cleanup

Dispatch explains scope and price before work starts. The main factors are below.

  • Water category, since contaminated water adds removal and sanitizing.
  • Number of rooms and how much debris must be hauled away.
  • Contents handling, such as moving and cleaning belongings.
  • Drying that follows, covered under water mitigation.
  • Repairs afterward, which are a separate step.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by insurance, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Check your policy. Photograph damaged items before they are discarded, and keep small samples of removed materials for the adjuster, as the Washington Insurance Commissioner advises.

A simple cleanup checklist while you wait

A few small steps protect your belongings before the crew arrives. None of them replace professional cleanup, and all of them are optional if the area is unsafe.

  1. Photograph the damage from several angles, including the source.
  2. Move dry belongings to a dry room, and set wet items on foil or plastic.
  3. Remove loose wet rugs and lift curtains off damp floors.
  4. Open interior doors for airflow if there is no active leak.
  5. Write down what was wet and when the water started, for your insurer.

Keep receipts for anything you spend on emergency supplies. Your insurer may reimburse reasonable costs under a covered loss, depending on your policy.

Cleanup before repair: why the order matters

Rebuilding over damp or contaminated materials locks the problem inside the wall. Crews clean and dry first, confirm readings, and only then hand the job to repair. A contractor who offers to repaint a still-wet wall is saving you a short-term headache and a long-term one.
